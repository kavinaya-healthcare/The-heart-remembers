    20-week fetal heart check

    Enter measurements from a routine 18–22 week four-chamber (and optionally three-vessel) view. Each result is compared with 131,150 normal pregnancies measured the same way by AI in the Danish 2026 study.

    Examples are hypothetical cases for teaching, not patients. Enter values in millimetres, at the moment the atrioventricular valves are closed.

    Definitions as in the Danish study: sphericity index = ventricular width ÷ length (higher = rounder); relative wall thickness = 2 × septum width ÷ LV width; width measured from endocardium to septum at the widest point.

    Simplified half-ellipsoid shapes for teaching. Why the shape changes (Crispi 2010, 2020): a fetal heart short of oxygen and nutrients may be unable to thicken its wall under extra load, so it adapts its geometry instead and becomes more globular.

    Why the heart turns round

    Laplace's law: wall stress rises with pressure and chamber radius, and falls with wall thickness (stress ∝ P × r ÷ 2h). Raise the placental load, then try the two ways a heart can respond.

    Wall stress

    Red line: normal stress (100). The bar uses the simple Laplace equation for pressure, radius and thickness.

    The shape slider is shown visually only. A rounder ventricle helps the fetal heart tolerate higher wall stress (Crispi 2020), but the exact effect depends on local wall curvature, which this simple equation does not capture.

    How the AI reads a scan

    What the 2026 Danish study's AI did with 139,430 routine 20-week scans, step by step. You measured one heart by hand; the AI does this for every chamber, the same way, in every scan.

    What the AI did not do: it did not diagnose or predict pre-eclampsia or growth restriction. It measured; the differences came from comparing those measurements across groups.
